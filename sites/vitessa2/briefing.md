# VITESSA (vitessamab) — Site Briefing

## Drug Profile

- **Brand name:** VITESSA
- **Generic name:** vitessamab
- **Class:** First-in-class anti-IL-33 monoclonal antibody (fully human IgG1)
- **Indication:** Add-on maintenance treatment in adults with severe eosinophilic asthma inadequately controlled despite high-dose inhaled corticosteroids plus a long-acting &beta;2-agonist (ICS/LABA)
- **Extended indication:** Oral corticosteroid-sparing treatment in adults with severe corticosteroid-dependent asthma
- **Brand colour:** Verdant Teal #0A7E8C
- **Approval:** MHRA approved, January 2026

## Mechanism of Action

Vitessamab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that binds to interleukin-33 (IL-33) and prevents its interaction with the ST2 receptor. IL-33 is an epithelial-derived alarmin released upon airway damage, infection, or allergen exposure. By neutralising IL-33 upstream of the inflammatory cascade, vitessamab:

- Suppresses type 2 inflammation by preventing activation of ILC2 cells, Th2 cells, mast cells, and eosinophils
- Reduces eosinophil recruitment and survival in the airways
- Attenuates mucus hypersecretion and airway remodelling driven by IL-33/ST2 signalling
- Preserves innate antiviral immune responses, unlike downstream cytokine blockers

Unlike anti-IL-5 or anti-IL-4R&alpha; approaches that target single downstream mediators, IL-33 blockade acts at the apex of the type 2 inflammatory cascade, addressing the initial alarmin signal that triggers multiple pathways simultaneously.

## Dosing

- **Formulation:** 210 mg/1.4 mL solution for injection in a pre-filled pen
- **Recommended dose:** 210 mg subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks
- **Administration:** Self-administered by the patient after appropriate training, via pre-filled pen into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm
- **No loading dose required**
- **Storage:** Refrigerate at 2&ndash;8&deg;C. May be stored at room temperature (up to 25&deg;C) for a maximum of 7 days. Do not re-refrigerate after room temperature storage.
- **No dose adjustment** required for body weight, age, or mild-to-moderate renal or hepatic impairment

## Clinical Programme: CLARITY

### CLARITY-1 (Phase III, pivotal &mdash; Exacerbation reduction)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre, 52 weeks
- **Population:** 1,061 adults with severe eosinophilic asthma (blood eosinophils &ge;300 cells/&mu;L), &ge;2 exacerbations in the prior 12 months, receiving high-dose ICS/LABA &plusmn; additional controllers
- **Primary endpoint:** Annualised asthma exacerbation rate (AAER) at Week 52
- **Key results:**
  - AAER: **0.28 vs 0.87** (68% reduction; rate ratio 0.32, 95% CI: 0.25&ndash;0.41; P&lt;0.001)
  - Pre-bronchodilator FEV1 change from baseline: **+280 mL vs +50 mL** (difference 230 mL; P&lt;0.001)
  - Time to first exacerbation: HR 0.37 (95% CI: 0.29&ndash;0.47; P&lt;0.001)
  - ACQ-6 responder rate (&ge;0.5-point improvement): 72.1% vs 48.6% (P&lt;0.001)
  - Blood eosinophil reduction from baseline: 85% vs 12% at Week 52
  - Exacerbations requiring hospitalisation or ED visit: 78% reduction (P&lt;0.001)

### CLARITY-2 (Phase III, pivotal &mdash; OCS-sparing)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre, 28 weeks
- **Population:** 421 adults with severe OCS-dependent asthma (maintenance prednisone/prednisolone &ge;5 mg/day for &ge;6 months), receiving high-dose ICS/LABA
- **Primary endpoint:** Percentage reduction in daily oral corticosteroid dose at Week 28 while maintaining asthma control
- **Key results:**
  - Median OCS dose reduction: **82% vs 50%** (P&lt;0.001)
  - Complete OCS elimination (0 mg/day): **58% vs 19%** (P&lt;0.001)
  - OCS dose &le;5 mg/day: 84% vs 54% (P&lt;0.001)
  - Maintained asthma control: no increase in exacerbations during OCS reduction
  - FEV1 improvement maintained despite OCS reduction: +190 mL vs &minus;20 mL (P&lt;0.001)

## Safety Profile (Pooled data, n=1,482)

### Most common adverse events (&ge;3%)

| Adverse Event | VITESSA (n=989) | Placebo (n=493) |
|---|---|---|
| Injection site reaction | 12.4% | 4.2% |
| Headache | 8.1% | 7.4% |
| Nasopharyngitis | 7.3% | 8.1% |
| Back pain | 4.2% | 3.8% |
| Herpes zoster | 2.8% | 0.4% |
| Fatigue | 2.6% | 2.4% |

### Key safety findings

- **Serious adverse events:** 8.2% vs 12.1% placebo (difference driven by fewer asthma-related hospitalisations)
- **Treatment discontinuation due to AEs:** 2.1% vs 3.4%
- **Infections:** Overall infection rates similar between groups (28.4% vs 29.1%). Herpes zoster was reported more frequently with VITESSA.
- **Malignancy:** No imbalance observed (0.3% vs 0.4%)
- **Anaphylaxis/systemic hypersensitivity:** 0.2% (2 events); both resolved with standard treatment
- **Helminth infections:** Patients with pre-existing helminth infections were excluded from clinical trials. Treat pre-existing infections before initiating VITESSA.
- **Immunogenicity:** Treatment-emergent anti-drug antibodies in 4.8% of patients; no impact on pharmacokinetics, efficacy, or safety

### Special populations

- **Pregnancy:** No adequate data. Animal studies did not reveal direct harmful effects. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Human IgG is excreted in breast milk. A risk to the breastfed infant cannot be excluded. A decision should be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or treatment.
- **Elderly (&ge;65 years):** No dose adjustment required. Limited data in patients &ge;75 years.
- **Adolescents (12&ndash;17 years):** Currently under investigation in CLARITY-3. Not yet approved for adolescents.

#### Section 2 &mdash; IL-33 Release (straight into content, no introduction)
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Image:** https://vitessa-images.pages.dev/epithelial-damage.jpeg (4:3, 800&times;600)

```
The alarmin signal: IL-33 release from damaged airways
When airway epithelial cells are damaged by allergens, respiratory viruses, or environmental pollutants, they release IL-33 as an alarm signal. Unlike cytokines that require de novo synthesis, IL-33 is pre-formed and released rapidly, acting as an immediate danger signal to the immune system.&sup1; &sup3;
IL-33 binds to the ST2 receptor on ILC2 cells, Th2 cells, mast cells, basophils, and eosinophils, initiating and amplifying the type 2 inflammatory response that drives severe eosinophilic asthma.&sup1;
```

#### Section 3 &mdash; Cascade Amplification
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Section metadata:** Style: highlight
**Image:** https://vitessa-images.pages.dev/cascade-amplification.jpeg (4:3, 800&times;600)

```
From one signal to many: the type 2 inflammatory cascade
Once released, IL-33 triggers a coordinated inflammatory response through multiple parallel pathways. It activates ILC2 cells to produce IL-5 and IL-13, drives Th2 cell differentiation, promotes eosinophil recruitment and survival, and stimulates mast cell degranulation.&sup1; &sup3;
This cascade results in the hallmark features of severe eosinophilic asthma: airway eosinophilia, mucus hypersecretion, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and progressive airway remodelling. Targeting downstream mediators individually leaves other branches of this cascade intact.&sup1;
```

#### Section 4 &mdash; VITESSA Mechanism
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Image:** https://vitessa-images.pages.dev/vitessa-blockade.jpeg (4:3, 800&times;600)

```
VITESSA: neutralising IL-33 at the apex of the cascade
Vitessamab binds to IL-33 with high affinity and prevents its interaction with the ST2 receptor, effectively silencing the alarmin signal before it can initiate downstream inflammation.&sup1;
By acting upstream of IL-5, IL-13, and IL-4 signalling, VITESSA simultaneously suppresses eosinophil activation, mucus production, and airway remodelling through a single mechanism. This broad anti-inflammatory effect is reflected in the clinical data: deep suppression of blood eosinophils (85% reduction), substantial lung function improvement (+280 mL FEV1), and dramatic exacerbation reduction (68%) in the CLARITY-1 trial.&sup1; &sup4;
[View CLARITY clinical data](/vitessa2/clarity-data)
```

Why IL-33 matters in severe asthma
IL-33 is released by damaged airway epithelial cells in response to allergens, viruses, pollutants, and mechanical stress. As the most upstream trigger of the type 2 inflammatory response, IL-33 activates multiple cell types and pathways simultaneously &mdash; making it a uniquely strategic therapeutic target.&sup1; &sup3;
