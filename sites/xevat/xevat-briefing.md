# Drug Profile

- **Brand name:** XEVAT
- **Generic name:** xevatirel
- **Class:** Selective oral JAK1 inhibitor (Janus kinase 1)
- **Indication:** Treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis (RA) who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs)
- **Extended indication:** Treatment of active psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in adults who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to a prior DMARD therapy
- **Brand colour:** Amber Teal #0D7377
- **Approval:** MHRA approved, January 2026

# Mechanism of Action

Xevatirel is a selective, reversible inhibitor of Janus kinase 1 (JAK1), a key intracellular signalling enzyme that mediates the activity of multiple pro-inflammatory cytokines implicated in the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis and psoriatic arthritis. By preferentially inhibiting JAK1 over JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2, xevatirel:

- Blocks IL-6, IL-7, IL-15, and IFN-gamma signalling --- key drivers of synovial inflammation and joint destruction
- Reduces T-cell activation and B-cell differentiation, attenuating autoimmune-driven tissue damage
- Preserves haematopoietic and immune homeostasis by minimising JAK2 and JAK3 inhibition
- Achieves rapid onset of action within days, with sustained disease control through continuous oral dosing

The selectivity ratio for JAK1 over JAK2 is approximately 30-fold, resulting in a favourable haematological safety profile while maintaining robust anti-inflammatory efficacy.

# Dosing

- **Formulation:** Film-coated tablets: 100 mg and 200 mg strengths
- **RA dose:** 200 mg once daily with or without food
- **PsA dose:** 200 mg once daily, in combination with a conventional synthetic DMARD (csDMARD)
- **Dose adjustment:** 100 mg once daily for patients with moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30-60 mL/min) or when co-administered with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors
- **Contraindication:** Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment or severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min)

# Clinical Programme: RESOLVE

## RESOLVE-1 (Phase III, pivotal --- RA, inadequate response to MTX)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre
- **Population:** 1,247 adults with moderately to severely active RA despite stable methotrexate (MTX), for ≥12 weeks
- **Primary endpoints:** ACR20 at Week 12 and change from baseline in DAS28-CRP at Week 24

### Key Results --- RESOLVE-1

| **Endpoint** | **XEVAT 200 mg + MTX (n=416)** | **Placebo + MTX (n=415)** | **Significance** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACR20 at Week 12 (primary) | 71.4% | 35.8% | P<0.001 |
| ACR50 at Week 12 | 43.2% | 14.6% | P<0.001 |
| ACR70 at Week 12 | 21.8% | 5.3% | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP change at Wk 24 (co-primary) | -2.89 | -1.24 | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP remission (<2.6) at Wk 24 | 38.7% | 9.2% | P<0.001 |
| HAQ-DI change at Week 12 | -0.62 | -0.28 | P<0.001 |
| Radiographic non-progression at Wk 52 | 89.3% | 71.6% | P<0.001 |

## RESOLVE-2 (Phase III --- RA, inadequate response to biologics)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre
- **Population:** 684 adults with active RA who had inadequate response or intolerance to ≥1 bDMARD
- **Primary endpoint:** ACR20 at Week 12

| **Endpoint** | **XEVAT 200 mg + MTX (n=342)** | **Placebo + MTX (n=342)** | **Significance** |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACR20 at Week 12 (primary) | 64.2% | 28.1% | P<0.001 |
| ACR50 at Week 12 | 35.7% | 11.9% | P<0.001 |
| ACR70 at Week 12 | 16.4% | 3.8% | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP change at Week 24 | -2.51 | -0.98 | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP remission (<2.6) at Wk 24 | 29.4% | 6.7% | P<0.001 |

# Safety Profile (Pooled data, n=1,931)

## Most common adverse events (≥5%)

| **Adverse Event** | **XEVAT (n=1,287)** | **Placebo (n=644)** |
|---|---|---|
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 14.2% | 11.8% |
| Nausea | 8.7% | 4.3% |
| Headache | 7.4% | 5.1% |
| Urinary tract infection | 5.8% | 4.6% |
| Elevated CPK | 5.3% | 1.2% |
| Herpes zoster | 3.8% | 0.9% |
| Neutropenia | 3.2% | 0.6% |

### Key safety findings

- Serious infections: 2.8% vs 2.1% (most common: pneumonia, cellulitis)
- Herpes zoster: 3.8% vs 0.9% (mostly non-serious, dermatomal)
- Venous thromboembolism: 0.4% vs 0.3% (no imbalance observed)
- Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE): 0.3% vs 0.2%
- Malignancy (excluding NMSC): 0.5% vs 0.4%
- Serious adverse events: 8.4% vs 7.9%
- Discontinuation due to AEs: 4.2% vs 3.1%

### Special populations

- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Effective contraception required during treatment and for 1 week after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended during treatment.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment required. No clinically meaningful differences in efficacy.
- **Renal impairment:** 100 mg once daily for moderate impairment. Not recommended in severe impairment.
- **Hepatic impairment:** Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment. No adjustment for mild-to-moderate.

### Section 4 --- Key Safety Considerations

**Block: columns-teaser | Section metadata: Style: highlight**

Important safety considerations
Infections: Serious infections occurred in 2.8% of patients. Do not initiate XEVAT during active, serious infections. Monitor for signs and symptoms of infection during and after treatment. Interrupt treatment if a serious infection develops.¹
Herpes zoster: Risk increased (3.8% vs 0.9%). Consider vaccination prior to starting treatment.¹
Haematological monitoring: Monitor neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, and haemoglobin at baseline and during treatment. Do not initiate if ANC <1,000/mm³, ALC <500/mm³, or Hb <8 g/dL.¹
Lipids: Monitor lipid parameters 12 weeks after initiation. Manage hyperlipidaemia per clinical guidelines.¹

### Section 2 --- JAK-STAT Pathway Introduction

**Block: introduction | Section metadata: Style: light**

The JAK-STAT signalling pathway
The Janus kinase (JAK)-Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription (STAT) pathway is a critical intracellular signalling cascade that mediates the biological effects of numerous cytokines, interferons, and growth factors involved in immune regulation, haematopoiesis, and inflammation.¹ ³

### Section 3 --- JAK1 in RA

**Block: columns-teaser**

JAK1: a central node in RA pathogenesis
JAK1 pairs with other JAK family members to transduce signals from IL-6, IL-7, IL-15, IFN-gamma, and other cytokines that drive the chronic synovial inflammation and joint destruction characteristic of RA. JAK1 inhibition attenuates these pathways, reducing inflammatory cell activation and joint damage.¹ ³

### Section 4 --- Selectivity Advantage

**Block: columns-teaser | Section metadata: Style: highlight**

The selectivity advantage
XEVAT demonstrates approximately 30-fold selectivity for JAK1 over JAK2. This selectivity is clinically meaningful: JAK2 mediates erythropoietin and thrombopoietin signalling, so minimising JAK2 inhibition reduces the risk of anaemia and thrombocytopenia.¹
JAK3 inhibition is similarly minimised, preserving aspects of adaptive immunity mediated by JAK3-dependent cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, IL-21).¹
