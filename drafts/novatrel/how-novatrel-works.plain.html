Targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint

NOVATREL™ (telvisoran) blocks the PD-1 immune checkpoint, restoring the anti-tumour immune response in patients with advanced NSCLC whose tumours exploit this pathway to evade immune detection.³

How tumours evade immune detection

In normal immune function, the PD-1 receptor on T cells helps regulate the immune response by preventing excessive tissue damage. However, tumour cells exploit this mechanism by upregulating PD-L1 on their surface, binding to PD-1 on tumour-infiltrating T cells and effectively switching off the anti-tumour immune response.³ &sup5;

In NSCLC tumours with high PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥50%), this immune checkpoint is the dominant mechanism of immune evasion, making these tumours particularly responsive to PD-1 pathway blockade.&sup5;

Reactivating the immune response with NOVATREL

Telvisoran binds with high affinity to PD-1, blocking its interaction with both PD-L1 and PD-L2. By preventing checkpoint engagement, NOVATREL reactivates exhausted tumour-infiltrating T cells, restoring their ability to:³

Recognise tumour-specific antigens presented on cancer cells. Proliferate and mount an effective cytotoxic T-cell response. Produce pro-inflammatory cytokines that recruit additional immune cells to the tumour. Establish immune memory, contributing to the durability of clinical responses observed in the TRAJECTORY programme.³

From immune reactivation to durable clinical benefit

The ability of NOVATREL to restore anti-tumour immune memory is reflected in the TRAJECTORY clinical data: 18% of patients who responded to NOVATREL maintained ongoing responses at 5 years, and the 5-year overall survival rate was 31.4%.² This durability distinguishes immunotherapy-driven responses from the transient benefit typically observed with cytotoxic chemotherapy.

Prescribing Information

NOVATREL (telvisoran) 200 mg/10 mL concentrate for solution for infusion. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. Indication: First-line monotherapy for adults with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC whose tumours express PD-L1 (TPS ≥50%), with no EGFR or ALK genomic tumour aberrations. Dosage: 200 mg by intravenous infusion over 30 minutes every 3 weeks, or 400 mg every 6 weeks. Treat until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to 24 months. Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to telvisoran or any excipient. Warnings and precautions: Immune-mediated adverse reactions may be severe or fatal. Monitor for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis, and skin reactions. Manage with dose interruption, corticosteroids, or permanent discontinuation as appropriate. Infusion-related reactions have been reported. Pregnancy and lactation: May cause foetal harm; effective contraception required during treatment and for 4 months after. Breastfeeding not recommended during treatment and for 4 months after. Side effects: Very common (≥1/10): fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, decreased appetite, rash, diarrhoea, nausea. Common (≥1/100 to <1/10): cough, pruritus, dyspnoea, constipation, pneumonitis, hypothyroidism. Legal category: POM. Pack and price: 1 vial (200 mg/10 mL): £2,630.00. Marketing authorisation holder: AstraZeneca UK Ltd. MA number: PLGB 17901/0512. Full prescribing information available from: AstraZeneca UK Ltd, 2 Pancras Square, London N1C 4AG.
