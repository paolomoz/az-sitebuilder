Important safety information

NOVATREL can cause immune-mediated adverse reactions, which may be severe or fatal, including pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis, and skin reactions. Monitor patients for signs and symptoms. Withhold or permanently discontinue NOVATREL as described in the SmPC.³ For full prescribing information, see below.

Median overall survival of 26.3 months

In TRAJECTORY-1, NOVATREL demonstrated a median overall survival of 26.3 months compared with 14.2 months for platinum-doublet chemotherapy in first-line advanced NSCLC with PD-L1 TPS ≥50%.¹

Durable responses lasting a median of 22.8 months

Nearly half of patients responded to NOVATREL (ORR 48%), with responses lasting a median of 22.8 months — more than three times longer than chemotherapy (6.3 months).¹

Explore response durability

5-year survival in nearly 1 in 3 patients

Extended follow-up of TRAJECTORY-1 demonstrated a 5-year overall survival rate of 31.4% with NOVATREL, compared with 14.6% with chemotherapy, with 18% of initial responders maintaining ongoing responses at 5 years.²

Advanced NSCLC: the need for durable first-line treatment

Non-small cell lung cancer accounts for approximately 85% of all lung cancers, and the majority of patients present with locally advanced or metastatic disease.&sup4; Among patients whose tumours express high levels of PD-L1 (TPS ≥50%), immunotherapy offers the potential for durable responses driven by the patient’s own immune system — but long-term survival remains an unmet need for many.&sup5;

Restoring anti-tumour immunity

Tumour cells exploit the PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint to suppress T-cell-mediated immune responses. NOVATREL blocks this interaction, reactivating the immune system’s ability to recognise and eliminate cancer cells.³

Prescribing Information

NOVATREL (telvisoran) 200 mg/10 mL concentrate for solution for infusion. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. Indication: First-line monotherapy for adults with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC whose tumours express PD-L1 (TPS ≥50%), with no EGFR or ALK genomic tumour aberrations. Dosage: 200 mg by intravenous infusion over 30 minutes every 3 weeks, or 400 mg every 6 weeks. Treat until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to 24 months. Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to telvisoran or any excipient. Warnings and precautions: Immune-mediated adverse reactions may be severe or fatal. Monitor for signs and symptoms of pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis, and skin reactions. Manage with dose interruption, corticosteroids, or permanent discontinuation as appropriate. Infusion-related reactions have been reported. Pregnancy and lactation: May cause foetal harm; effective contraception required during treatment and for 4 months after. Breastfeeding not recommended during treatment and for 4 months after. Side effects: Very common (≥1/10): fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, decreased appetite, rash, diarrhoea, nausea. Common (≥1/100 to <1/10): cough, pruritus, dyspnoea, constipation, pneumonitis, hypothyroidism. Legal category: POM. Pack and price: 1 vial (200 mg/10 mL): £2,630.00. Marketing authorisation holder: AstraZeneca UK Ltd. MA number: PLGB 17901/0512. Full prescribing information available from: AstraZeneca UK Ltd, 2 Pancras Square, London N1C 4AG.
