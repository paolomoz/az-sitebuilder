## 2. Mechanism of Action

Zenturigliptin is a first-in-class oral, non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist. Unlike injectable GLP-1 receptor agonists that are peptide-based and require subcutaneous administration, zenturigliptin is a small molecule designed for oral bioavailability through a novel absorption-enhancing formulation.

Zenturigliptin binds to and activates the GLP-1 receptor on pancreatic beta cells, stimulating glucose-dependent insulin secretion and suppressing inappropriately elevated glucagon secretion. It also slows gastric emptying and promotes satiety through central mechanisms, contributing to weight reduction.

The glucose-dependent mechanism of insulin secretion means the risk of hypoglycaemia is inherently low when used as monotherapy or in combination with metformin.

## 3. Clinical Programme

### ZENITH-1: Glycaemic control vs placebo (add-on to metformin)

- **Design**: Phase III, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52-week trial
- **Population**: 1,246 adults with T2DM inadequately controlled on metformin (HbA1c 7.0--10.5%)
- **Primary endpoint**: Change in HbA1c from baseline at Week 26
- **Key results**:
  - HbA1c change from baseline: **−1.4%** (zenturigliptin) vs **−0.3%** (placebo); estimated treatment difference **−1.1%** (95% CI: −1.3 to −0.9; P\<0.001)
  - Proportion achieving HbA1c \<7.0%: **72%** vs **28%** (P\<0.001)
  - Body weight change: **−5.2 kg** vs **−1.1 kg**; difference **−4.1 kg** (95% CI: −4.8 to −3.4; P\<0.001)
  - Fasting plasma glucose change: **−2.1 mmol/L** vs **−0.4 mmol/L** (P\<0.001)
- **Publication**: Harrison et al. *Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol* 2025; 13(4): 312--325.

### ZENITH-2: Non-inferiority vs injectable semaglutide 1 mg

- **Design**: Phase III, randomised, open-label, active-controlled, 52-week trial
- **Population**: 1,834 adults with T2DM inadequately controlled on metformin ± sulfonylurea (HbA1c 7.0--10.5%)
- **Primary endpoint**: Change in HbA1c from baseline at Week 52, non-inferiority margin 0.3%
- **Key results**:
  - HbA1c change from baseline: **−1.3%** (zenturigliptin) vs **−1.4%** (semaglutide 1 mg); estimated treatment difference **0.1%** (95% CI: −0.1 to 0.2); non-inferiority confirmed (P\<0.001)
  - Proportion achieving HbA1c \<7.0%: **68%** vs **71%**
  - Body weight change: **−4.8 kg** vs **−5.5 kg**
  - Patient preference (pre-specified exploratory): **81%** preferred oral administration
  - Treatment satisfaction (DTSQ): significantly higher with zenturigliptin (P=0.003)
- **Publication**: Chen et al. *N Engl J Med* 2025; 392(18): 1697--1710.

### ZENITH-3: Cardiovascular outcomes trial

- **Design**: Phase III, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, event-driven cardiovascular outcomes trial
- **Population**: 8,246 adults with T2DM and established cardiovascular disease or multiple cardiovascular risk factors
- **Median follow-up**: 3.2 years
- **Primary endpoint**: First occurrence of MACE (composite of cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke)
- **Key results**:
  - MACE: HR **0.82** (95% CI: 0.70--0.96; P=0.013) --- 18% relative risk reduction
  - Cardiovascular death: HR **0.78** (95% CI: 0.62--0.98; P=0.031)
  - Non-fatal MI: HR **0.86** (95% CI: 0.69--1.07; P=0.18)
  - Non-fatal stroke: HR **0.81** (95% CI: 0.60--1.10; P=0.18)
  - All-cause mortality: HR **0.85** (95% CI: 0.71--1.02; P=0.08)
- **Publication**: Mehta et al. *N Engl J Med* 2025; 393(2): 134--148.

### Key safety findings

- **Gastrointestinal events**: Most common class of AEs. Typically mild to moderate in severity, occurring within the first 4--8 weeks of treatment and declining with continued use. Discontinuation due to GI events: 4.2%.
- **Hepatic effects**: Transient ALT elevations \>3× ULN occurred in 3.1% of patients. Liver function tests should be performed prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Discontinue if ALT \>3× ULN accompanied by symptoms or bilirubin elevation.
- **Hypoglycaemia**: Low risk as monotherapy or with metformin (documented hypoglycaemia: 1.2%). Risk increased when co-administered with sulfonylureas (7.8%) or insulin (12.4%). Dose reduction of concomitant sulfonylurea or insulin may be required.
- **Pancreatitis**: Acute pancreatitis reported in 0.1% of patients. Discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected; do not restart.

### Special populations

- **Renal impairment**: No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30--89 mL/min/1.73m²). Not recommended in severe renal impairment (eGFR \<30) or end-stage renal disease due to limited data.
- **Hepatic impairment**: No dose adjustment in mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A). Caution in moderate impairment (Child-Pugh B). Not recommended in severe impairment (Child-Pugh C).
- **Elderly (≥65 years)**: No dose adjustment required. Clinical experience in patients ≥75 years is limited.

## 5. Prescribing Information (abbreviated --- for every page footer)

ZENTURIS (zenturigliptin) 14 mg film-coated tablets. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. **Indication:** Treatment of adults with insufficiently controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus as an adjunct to diet and exercise. **Dosage and administration:** 14 mg once weekly, taken in the morning on an empty stomach with a sip of plain water. The tablet must be swallowed whole and not crushed, chewed, or split. No food, drink, or other oral medicinal products should be taken for at least 30 minutes after administration. **Contraindications:** Hypersensitivity to zenturigliptin or any excipient. ------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Section 2 --- How to take Zenturis (two-column: image left, text right) \| light background

**Image:**

![Administration](media/rId84.jpg){width="4.0in" height="2.986666666666667in"} `https://zenturis-images.pages.dev/columns-admin.jpeg`

**Heading:** How to take Zenturis

**Body text:** Zenturigliptin 14 mg should be taken **once weekly**, on the same day each week, in the morning on an empty stomach.⁴

The tablet should be swallowed whole with a sip of plain water (up to 120 mL). Patients should not eat, drink, or take other oral medicinal products for at least 30 minutes after taking zenturigliptin.⁴

The tablet must not be crushed, chewed, or split, as this may affect absorption.⁴

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Section 3 --- Dosing schedule and missed doses (two-column: image left, text right)

**Image:**

![Dosing schedule](media/rId88.jpg){width="4.0in" height="2.986666666666667in"} `https://zenturis-images.pages.dev/columns-dose-adjust.jpeg`

**Heading:** Dosing schedule and missed doses

**Body text:** **Fixed dose:** Zenturigliptin is prescribed at a fixed dose of 14 mg once weekly. No dose titration is required.⁴

**Missed dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible, provided there are at least 3 days (72 hours) before the next scheduled dose. If fewer than 3 days remain, the missed dose should be skipped and the next dose taken on the regularly scheduled day.⁴

**Day change:** The day of weekly administration may be changed, provided there are at least 3 days between two doses.⁴

------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Section 4 --- Use with other diabetes medicines (centred text) \| highlighted background

**Heading:** Use with other diabetes medicines

**Body text:** Zenturigliptin can be used as monotherapy (when metformin is not tolerated) or in combination with metformin, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors, or basal insulin. When combined with a sulfonylurea or insulin, a dose reduction of the sulfonylurea or insulin may be required to reduce the risk of hypoglycaemia. Zenturigliptin should not be used in combination with other GLP-1 receptor agonists or DPP-4 inhibitors.⁴
