## 2. Mechanism of Action

Xevatirel is a selective, reversible inhibitor of Janus kinase 1 (JAK1), a key intracellular signalling enzyme that mediates the activity of multiple pro-inflammatory cytokines implicated in the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis and psoriatic arthritis.¹ By preferentially inhibiting JAK1 over JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2, xevatirel:

- Blocks IL-6, IL-7, IL-15, and IFN-gamma signalling — key drivers of synovial inflammation and joint destruction¹ ³
- Reduces T-cell activation and B-cell differentiation, attenuating autoimmune-driven tissue damage¹
- Preserves haematopoietic and immune homeostasis by minimising JAK2 and JAK3 inhibition¹
- Achieves rapid onset of action within days, with sustained disease control through continuous oral dosing¹

The selectivity ratio for JAK1 over JAK2 is approximately 30-fold, resulting in a favourable haematological safety profile while maintaining robust anti-inflammatory efficacy.¹

## 3. Clinical Programme: RESOLVE

### RESOLVE-1 (Phase III, pivotal — RA, inadequate response to MTX)

- Design: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre
- Population: 1,247 adults with moderately to severely active RA despite stable methotrexate (MTX) for ≥12 weeks
- Primary endpoints: ACR20 at Week 12 and change from baseline in DAS28-CRP at Week 24
- Publication: RESOLVE-1 Study Group. N Engl J Med. 2025;392(18):1712-1724.³

| Endpoint | XEVAT 200 mg + MTX (n=416) | Placebo + MTX (n=415) | Significance |
|----------|---------------------------|-----------------------|--------------|
| ACR20 at Week 12 (primary) | 71.4% | 35.8% | P<0.001 |
| ACR50 at Week 12 | 43.2% | 14.6% | P<0.001 |
| ACR70 at Week 12 | 21.8% | 5.3% | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP change at Wk 24 (co-primary) | -2.89 | -1.24 | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP remission (<2.6) at Wk 24 | 38.7% | 9.2% | P<0.001 |
| HAQ-DI change at Week 12 | -0.62 | -0.28 | P<0.001 |
| Radiographic non-progression at Wk 52 | 89.3% | 71.6% | P<0.001 |

### RESOLVE-2 (Phase III — RA, inadequate response to biologics)

- Design: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre
- Population: 684 adults with active RA who had inadequate response or intolerance to ≥1 bDMARD
- Primary endpoint: ACR20 at Week 12
- Publication: RESOLVE-2 Study Group. Ann Rheum Dis. 2025;84(9):1289-1301.⁴

| Endpoint | XEVAT 200 mg + MTX (n=342) | Placebo + MTX (n=342) | Significance |
|----------|---------------------------|-----------------------|--------------|
| ACR20 at Week 12 (primary) | 64.2% | 28.1% | P<0.001 |
| ACR50 at Week 12 | 35.7% | 11.9% | P<0.001 |
| ACR70 at Week 12 | 16.4% | 3.8% | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP change at Week 24 | -2.51 | -0.98 | P<0.001 |
| DAS28-CRP remission (<2.6) at Wk 24 | 29.4% | 6.7% | P<0.001 |

## 4. Safety Profile (Pooled data, n=1,931)

### Most common adverse events (≥5% incidence)

| Adverse Event | XEVAT (n=1,287) | Placebo (n=644) |
|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 14.2% | 11.8% |
| Nausea | 8.7% | 4.3% |
| Headache | 7.4% | 5.1% |
| Urinary tract infection | 5.8% | 4.6% |
| Elevated CPK | 5.3% | 1.2% |
| Herpes zoster | 3.8% | 0.9% |
| Neutropenia | 3.2% | 0.6% |

### Key safety findings

- Serious infections: 2.8% vs 2.1% (most common: pneumonia, cellulitis)
- Herpes zoster: 3.8% vs 0.9% (mostly non-serious, dermatomal) — consider vaccination prior to initiation
- Venous thromboembolism: 0.4% vs 0.3% (no imbalance observed)
- Major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE): 0.3% vs 0.2%
- Malignancy (excluding NMSC): 0.5% vs 0.4%
- Serious adverse events: 8.4% vs 7.9%
- Discontinuation due to AEs: 4.2% vs 3.1%

### Monitoring

- Full blood count: baseline, after 4 weeks, then every 3 months
- Liver function: baseline, then as clinically indicated. Interrupt if ALT/AST >5x ULN
- Lipids: at 12 weeks after initiation; manage hyperlipidaemia per clinical guidelines
- Viral hepatitis: screen for hepatitis B and C before starting treatment

### Special populations

- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Effective contraception required during treatment and for 1 week after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended during treatment.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal impairment:** 100 mg once daily for moderate impairment (eGFR 30–60 mL/min). Not recommended in severe impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic impairment:** Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment. No adjustment for mild-to-moderate.
- **CYP3A4 interaction:** Reduce dose to 100 mg once daily with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors.

#### Section 3 — JAK1 in RA
Block: `columns-teaser`

- Heading: JAK1 — a central node in RA pathogenesis
- Body: JAK1 pairs with other JAK family members to transduce signals from IL-6, IL-7, IL-15, IFN-gamma, and other cytokines that drive chronic synovial inflammation and joint destruction. JAK1 inhibition attenuates these pathways, reducing inflammatory cell activation and joint damage.¹ ²

#### Section 4 — Selectivity advantage
Block: `columns-teaser`
Section metadata: Style: highlight

- Heading: The selectivity advantage
- Body: XEVAT demonstrates approximately 30-fold selectivity for JAK1 over JAK2. JAK2 mediates erythropoietin and thrombopoietin signalling, so minimising JAK2 inhibition reduces the risk of anaemia and thrombocytopenia. JAK3 inhibition is similarly minimised, preserving aspects of adaptive immunity mediated by JAK3-dependent cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, IL-21).¹
