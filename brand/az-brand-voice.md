# AstraZeneca UK — Brand Voice & Imagery Skill

> Derived from visual and textual analysis of 16 live myastrazeneca.co.uk pages, February 2026.
> Use this file as the authoritative guide when writing copy or selecting imagery for any AZ UK HCP website.

---

## 1. Brand Voice Overview

AstraZeneca UK speaks to **Healthcare Professionals** (doctors, nurses, pharmacists) as a trusted scientific partner. The voice is never casual, never salesy, never patronising. It balances **clinical authority** with **human warmth** — the company positions itself as standing alongside HCPs, not above them.

### Core Voice Attributes

| Attribute | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Supportive** | Positions AZ as a partner to HCPs, not a vendor | "How can we help you today?" |
| **Evidence-led** | Every claim is referenced; data speaks first | "56% reduction in exacerbations (P<0.001)" |
| **Action-oriented** | Drives towards clinical decisions | "Explore efficacy data", "See dosing guide" |
| **Measured urgency** | Conveys importance without alarm | "A growing concern", "The scale of the challenge" |
| **Precise** | Clinical language used correctly; no hedging | "indicated for", "significantly reduces" |

### What AZ Voice Is NOT

- Not consumer-facing — never dumbs down clinical terminology
- Not fear-based — presents challenges factually, never dramatises
- Not promotional in tone — lets data and evidence make the case
- Not informal — no contractions in clinical copy, no colloquialisms
- Not self-congratulatory — centres the patient and HCP, not the company

## 2. Voice by Page Type

### 2.1 Homepage / Portal

**Register**: Warm, welcoming, invitational

The homepage is the only place AZ speaks in first person directly to the HCP as a host. The tone is that of a colleague opening a door.

**Patterns observed**:
- "Welcome to MyAstraZeneca."
- "How can we help you today?"
- "If you have any questions about AstraZeneca medicines or would like to speak to our representative, please contact us."

**Rules**:
- Use questions to invite engagement
- Keep headlines short and human
- CTA labels use soft action verbs: "Review", "Learn more about", "Explore our work in", "Understand our work in", "See the latest in"
- No product claims on the homepage — therapy areas only

### 2.2 Therapy Area Pages (Respiratory, Cardiovascular, Oncology)

**Register**: Authoritative, evidence-driven, measured urgency

These pages establish the disease burden, then position AZ as committed to solving it. The structure is always: **problem → scale → AZ commitment → products as solutions**.

**Heading patterns**:
- H1: Bold statement framing the challenge — "Transforming respiratory conditions in the UK", "Cardiovascular disease: a growing concern", "Oncology"
- H2: "Our [therapy] focus areas" / "The scale of the challenge" / "Our commitment to helping HCPs improve patient outcomes"

**Body copy patterns**:
- Opens with epidemiological context, always referenced: "Respiratory conditions, particularly COPD and asthma, profoundly affect the lives of millions in the UK."
- Uses specific UK statistics with superscript references: "around 3 million people", "200,000 new diagnoses every year", "550,000 people are diagnosed"
- Transitions to AZ commitment with "At AstraZeneca, we are dedicated to:" followed by bullet points
- Bullet points use present participle verbs: "Reducing asthma attacks through better management", "Slowing COPD disease progression", "Supporting early diagnosis", "Empowering healthcare professionals", "Addressing health inequalities"
- Closes with forward-looking research statement

**Rules**:
- Always cite statistics with superscript reference numbers
- Use UK-specific data (NHS, NICE, MHRA sources)
- Frame AZ as a partner in the healthcare system, not a standalone actor
- Product mentions are contextual, not promotional — "Explore AstraZeneca's options for managing [condition]:"
- Commitment statements use "we" and active verbs

### 2.3 Product Pages — Standard

**Register**: Clinical, precise, benefit-led

Product pages lead with the therapeutic benefit, not the product name. The drug is always presented as a tool for the HCP, not a product to be sold.

**Heading patterns**:
- H1 leads with clinical benefit: "Discover the cardiorenal protective benefits of Forxiga (dapagliflozin)", "Flu protection without the injection", "Discover the benefits of WAINZUA", "Discover Symbicort® (budesonide/formoterol) Turbohaler®"
- H2 sections: "About [generic name]", "[Product] is indicated for:", "Safety profile", "Resources for you and your patients"

**Body copy patterns**:
- Opens with the licensed indication, using exact SmPC language: "[Product] is indicated for the treatment of..."
- Dosing presented factually: "Simple once-daily dosing. Start on 10 mg daily, stay on 10 mg daily, no need for up-titration"
- Benefits framed as patient outcomes: "What WAINZUA could do for your eligible patients"
- Safety section always includes: "Addresses key safety considerations regarding the use of [product] in patients"
- Resources framed as support: "Explore informative videos and guides to help you understand more about [product] and support your patients"

**CTA patterns** (by product type):
- Access [condition] Patient Resources
- Learn more / Find out more
- See the data / View efficacy data
- How to prescribe [product]
- Access support resources
- Download this resource
- Order materials / Order now

**Rules**:
- Always include ® or ®▼ (black triangle) on first mention of product name
- Generic name in parentheses after brand name on first mention: "FORXIGA® (dapagliflozin)"
- Indication statements must match SmPC wording exactly
- Dosing information references SmPC: "Please refer to the full Summary of Product Characteristics"
- Never make comparative efficacy claims without trial data and references

### 2.4 Product Pages — Data-Forward (Oncology)

**Register**: Bold, data-driven, high-impact

Oncology products (Tezspire, Tagrisso, Imfinzi) use a more assertive voice with large visual statistics and imperative CTAs.

**Heading patterns**:
- H1 uses metaphorical/aspirational language: "Rise above the complexity with TEZSPIRE®", "Setting the standard in advanced EGFRm NSCLC"
- H2 leads with the key claim: "TEZSPIRE significantly reduces exacerbations in a broad population...", "TEZSPIRE treats across phenotypes and irrespective of key clinical biomarker levels"
- Key words highlighted in magenta bold within sentences: "**significantly** reduces", "treats across **phenotypes**"

**Data presentation patterns**:
- Large percentage with arrow: "56%" / "71%" with downward arrows
- Trial name as label above the statistic: "Navigator" / "Pathway"
- Full statistical context below: "RR=0.44, 95% CI: 0.37-0.53; P<0.001"
- CTAs in uppercase: "GET EFFICACY DATA", "EXPLORE DATA", "EXPLORE MOA"

**Rules**:
- Statistics must always include: rate ratio or hazard ratio, confidence interval, p-value, and trial name
- Use asterisks/daggers for footnotes explaining patient populations and study design
- Trial indication cards: heading (indication), brief SmPC-language description, "Learn more" CTA
- Comparative claims require "versus" with specific comparator (e.g., "vs standard of care", "vs placebo")

### 2.5 Fluenz (Childhood Vaccine — Unique Voice)

**Register**: Supportive, practical, programme-focused

Fluenz is the only product targeting a public health programme (childhood flu vaccination), so the voice is distinctly warmer and more operational.

**Patterns**:
- "Flu protection without the injection"
- "Find out more about how the childhood flu National Immunisation Programme can help protect children"
- "Explore a suite of print and digital resources for the teams delivering the childhood flu National Immunisation Programme"
- "Order stickers which children can receive after they are vaccinated"
- CTA language is practical: "Watch the video", "Order materials", "Request a call back", "Visit the website"

## 3. Sentence-Level Patterns

### 3.1 Sentence Structure

**Opening sentences** are typically declarative and grounded:
- "[Product] is indicated for the treatment of [condition] in [patient population]."
- "[Condition] profoundly affect[s] the lives of millions in the UK."
- "We are committed to improving treatment and research in [therapy area]."

**Benefit sentences** put the outcome first:
- "Simple once-daily dosing."
- "No requirement for dose titration in the majority of patients."
- "Once-monthly WAINZUA significantly lowers serum TTR levels, reduces neuropathy impairment and improves quality of life."

**Call-to-action sentences** use imperatives or soft invitations:
- "Explore AstraZeneca's options for managing [condition]."
- "Access prescribing information, dosing guides, patient support materials."
- "Watch this video to learn about the mechanism of action."

### 3.2 Vocabulary Preferences

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "indicated for" | "used to treat" |
| "demonstrated" / "achieved" | "showed" / "got" |
| "significantly reduces" | "greatly lowers" |
| "adult patients" | "adults" (in clinical context) |
| "healthcare professionals" | "doctors" (when generic) |
| "progression-free survival" | Simplified alternatives |
| "explore" / "discover" | "check out" / "see" |
| "resources" | "materials" / "stuff" |
| "managing" / "management" | "dealing with" |
| "support" / "supporting" | "help" (in HCP context) |

### 3.3 Trademark & Regulatory Language Conventions

- **First mention**: PRODUCT® (generic name) — e.g., "FORXIGA® (dapagliflozin)"
- **Black triangle products**: PRODUCT®▼ — e.g., "TEZSPIRE®▼ (tezepelumab)"
- **Subsequent mentions**: PRODUCT or product name without ® in flowing text
- **Approval codes**: "GB-XXXXX | DOP: Month Year" — always at bottom of page
- **Prescribing Information**: Full list of all product PIs, linked, near bottom of page
- **Abbreviations**: Defined in a dedicated section, always expandable/collapsible
- **References**: Superscript numbers in text, full citations in expandable references section
- **AE reporting boilerplate**: Always present, verbatim:
  > "Adverse events should be reported. Reporting forms and information can be found at www.mhra.gov.uk/yellowcard or search for MHRA Yellow Card in the Google Play or Apple App Store. Adverse events should also be reported to AstraZeneca by visiting https://contactazmedical.astrazeneca.com/ or by calling 0800 783 0033."

### 3.4 Heading Capitalisation

- H1: Sentence case — "Cardiovascular disease: a growing concern"
- H2: Sentence case — "Our commitment to helping HCPs improve patient outcomes"
- Product names: ALL CAPS in data-forward pages (TEZSPIRE, TAGRISSO), Title case elsewhere (Forxiga, Symbicort)
- CTA buttons: Sentence case for standard ("Learn more", "See the data"), ALL CAPS for high-impact ("GET EFFICACY DATA", "EXPLORE MOA")

## 4. Imagery Guidelines

### 4.1 Three Imagery Tiers

AZ UK uses three distinct categories of imagery, each serving a specific purpose. The choice depends on page type and context.

#### Tier 1: Double-Exposure Artistic Portraits (Signature AZ Visual)

**Where used**: Homepage cards, therapy area heroes
**What it is**: Human silhouettes (head/shoulders or full body) filled with scientific or medical imagery — molecules, cells, anatomical structures, natural landscapes. The human form contains the science.

**Visual characteristics**:
- Subject is typically in profile or three-quarter view
- Interior fill uses vibrant, saturated colors related to the therapy: blue/teal for respiratory (lungs, air), pink/red for cardiovascular (heart, vessels), warm magenta/purple for oncology
- Background is clean white or very light
- High contrast between the silhouette edge and the interior imagery
- Editorial quality — looks like a magazine cover, not stock art
- No text overlaid on the image itself

**Emotional tone**: Aspirational, visionary, science-as-beauty. Communicates that AZ sees the human being within the science.

**Prompt guidance for AI image generation**:
- "Double exposure portrait, human silhouette filled with [scientific imagery], clean white background, editorial pharmaceutical style, vibrant [therapy color], high contrast"
- Do NOT use: generic stock poses, smiling-at-camera portraits, lab coat imagery

#### Tier 2: Warm Lifestyle Photography

**Where used**: Product hero sections, patient benefit cards, resource sections
**What it is**: Natural-light photographs of diverse patients in positive, everyday settings. Not clinical — aspirational.

**Visual characteristics**:
- **Forxiga**: Warm, golden-hour lighting. Older adult (60s-70s) smiling warmly, wearing glasses, casual clothing. Feels like a portrait of someone's grandmother/grandfather. Orange/amber warmth matches brand color.
- **Symbicort**: Active lifestyle. Person jogging outdoors, natural movement. "With you every step of the way" positioning.
- **Wainzua**: Person walking in a park/garden. Green, natural environment. Matches the green brand color. "MOVE FORWARD" campaign imagery.
- **Fluenz**: Children and parents together. Bright, cheerful. Child receiving nasal spray. School/playground settings. Sky blue warmth.

**Rules**:
- Subjects are diverse in age, ethnicity, and gender
- Settings are always everyday environments (home, outdoors, garden) — NEVER hospitals, clinics, or labs
- Lighting is warm and natural — never harsh or clinical
- Subjects are active or engaged, not passive or suffering
- Clothing is casual/everyday, never medical gowns or formal wear
- Expression is positive but not exaggerated — peaceful, content, engaged

**Prompt guidance**:
- "Warm natural-light photograph, [age] [ethnicity] [gender] patient, [activity] in [everyday setting], [product brand color] warmth, editorial pharmaceutical lifestyle, positive but natural expression"
- Do NOT use: hospital settings, medical equipment visible, isolation/sadness, overly staged poses

#### Tier 3: Product & Device Photography

**Where used**: Product feature sections, dosing information, inhaler guides
**What it is**: Clean, professional shots of pharmaceutical devices — inhalers, pre-filled pens, sachets, packaging.

**Visual characteristics**:
- **Symbicort Turbohaler**: Red/white inhaler shown from multiple angles, product packaging visible, clean white background
- **Trixeo/Bevespi Aerosphere**: pMDI inhaler device, often with cutaway or detail view showing the delivery technology. Teal/mint brand color accents.
- **Lokelma**: Sachet illustration/diagram showing how to prepare the oral suspension
- **Wainzua**: Pre-filled pen device, shown in-hand for scale

**Rules**:
- Always shot on white or very light neutral background
- Product must be clearly identifiable (label visible)
- Multiple angles/views encouraged for device products
- Can include hands demonstrating use, but no full faces
- Brand color accents in styling/props are acceptable

#### Tier 4: Dramatic/Abstract Hero Imagery (Oncology Only)

**Where used**: Tezspire hero, Tagrisso hero
**What it is**: Bold, high-impact photography or CGI that communicates breakthrough and transformation.

**Visual characteristics**:
- **Tezspire**: Aurora borealis / fire / dramatic sky imagery. Dark background transitioning to warm orange/gold. Communicates "rising above" and transcendence.
- **Tagrisso**: Purple/teal gradient with lung anatomy imagery. Darker, more clinical but still dramatic.
- **Imfinzi**: Landscape photography (mountains, seas, vast horizons) behind indication cards. Communicates scale and possibility.

**Rules**:
- Reserved for oncology and severe disease products only — never for lifestyle products
- Must convey aspiration and breakthrough, not fear
- Can serve as background for overlay text/cards
- Color palette should relate to product brand color

### 4.2 Icon System

**Where used**: Product benefit grids, feature cards, resource sections (Lokelma, Wainzua, Trixeo)

**Visual characteristics**:
- **Shape**: Perfect circle, large (~80px diameter on desktop)
- **Style**: Flat, minimal, single-color
- **Color**: Product brand color background, white icon symbol
  - Lokelma: Teal (#00827F) circles with white symbols (shield, play button, medicine sachet)
  - Wainzua: Forest green circles with white symbols (heart, nerve, molecule)
  - Trixeo: Mint/teal circles
- **Symbols**: Simple, recognisable — shield for safety, play button for video, clipboard for resources, arrows for data

**Rules**:
- Always use product brand color, never AZ magenta for icons
- One icon per card/feature — never stack icons
- Keep symbols to 1-2 visual elements (not detailed illustrations)

### 4.3 Imagery Do's and Don'ts

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Show patients in positive, active situations | Show patients in distress or receiving treatment |
| Use warm, natural lighting | Use cold, clinical, fluorescent lighting |
| Include diverse representation | Default to single-demographic imagery |
| Let images breathe with white space around them | Crowd images with text overlays or busy layouts |
| Match image color temperature to product brand | Use generic stock imagery unrelated to the brand |
| Use editorial-quality, art-directed photography | Use obviously AI-generated or hyper-perfect images |
| Show real-world environments (home, nature) | Show lab/hospital/sterile environments |
| For oncology: use dramatic, aspirational imagery | For oncology: use dark, foreboding imagery |
| For devices: show clean product shots | For devices: show products in messy settings |

### 4.4 Image Sourcing Guidelines for Demo/Prototype

When building demo pages, source imagery that matches the AZ visual identity:

**For double-exposure artistic portraits**: Search Unsplash for "double exposure portrait science" or "silhouette filled with nature". If unavailable, use AI generation with careful prompting (see Tier 1 above).

**For lifestyle photography**: Use Unsplash with search terms like:
- "elderly person smiling outdoors warm light"
- "patient walking garden"
- "active senior lifestyle"
- "parent child healthcare"
- "diverse people everyday"

**For product/device shots**: Use placeholder rectangles with product name text if no real device photos available.

**For dramatic/abstract heroes**: Search Unsplash for "aurora borealis", "dramatic sky", "mountain landscape", "fire abstract".

**For icons**: Use a simple SVG icon library (Heroicons, Lucide) rendered in product brand color circles.

## 6. Copy Templates

### 6.1 Hero Section Template

```
[H1] [Benefit-led headline for product/therapy area]
[Paragraph] [Product] is indicated for [exact SmPC indication language].
[CTA] [Verb] [object] — e.g., "Explore efficacy data"
```

Example:
> **ZENVARA® (zenvatinib)**
> A first-in-class selective TKR inhibitor for the treatment of advanced non-small cell lung cancer in adult patients with confirmed EGFR T790M mutation.
> **Explore efficacy data →**

### 6.2 Therapy Area Introduction Template

```
[H1] [Challenge-framing headline — e.g., "Condition: a growing concern"]
[Paragraph] [Epidemiological context with UK statistics, 2-3 sentences, all referenced]
[H2] Our [therapy] focus areas
[Paragraph] We are committed to [improving/advancing] [therapy area].
[Accordion items] [Disease area 1] → [Disease area 2] → ...
[H2] The scale of the challenge
[Paragraph] [More detailed statistics, 3-5 sentences, all referenced]
[H2] Our commitment to helping HCPs improve patient outcomes
[Bullet list] At AstraZeneca, we are dedicated to:
  - [Present participle] [commitment 1]
  - [Present participle] [commitment 2]
  - [Present participle] [commitment 3]
```

### 6.3 Product Indication Card Template

```
[Strong] [Indication name — e.g., "Efficacy & Clinical Trials"]
[Paragraph] [1-2 sentence benefit statement with reference]
[CTA link] [Action verb] [object] — e.g., "View efficacy data"
```

### 6.4 Data Callout Template

```
[Trial name] — e.g., "HORIZON"
[Large statistic] — e.g., "42%"
[Arrow icon] — downward for reduction, upward for improvement
[Statistical context] — e.g., "HR 0.58, 95% CI: 0.47-0.72; P<0.001"
[Footnote] — patient population and study design
[CTA] — e.g., "GET EFFICACY DATA"
```

### 6.5 Regulatory Footer Template

```
[H2] Adverse event reporting
[Paragraph] Adverse events should be reported. Reporting forms and information can be found at www.mhra.gov.uk/yellowcard. Adverse events should also be reported to AstraZeneca by visiting contactazmedical.astrazeneca.com or by calling 0800 783 0033.

[H3] Prescribing Information
[Paragraph] [PRODUCT] ([generic]) Prescribing Information. Please refer to the full Summary of Product Characteristics before prescribing.
[Paragraph] GB-XXXXX | Date of Preparation: [Month Year]
```

## 7. Quick Reference: Writing Checklist

When writing copy for an AZ UK page, verify:

- [ ] All product names use correct trademark symbols (® or ®▼)
- [ ] Generic name follows brand name in parentheses on first mention
- [ ] All clinical claims have superscript reference numbers
- [ ] Indication language matches SmPC exactly
- [ ] Statistics include: metric, comparator, CI, p-value, trial name
- [ ] Headings are sentence case (not Title Case)
- [ ] CTA labels start with an action verb
- [ ] AE reporting boilerplate is present verbatim
- [ ] Approval code (GB-XXXXX | DOP: Month Year) is present
- [ ] Abbreviations are defined in a dedicated section
- [ ] Voice matches the page type (warm/portal, authoritative/therapy, clinical/product, bold/data-forward)
- [ ] No consumer-facing language — audience is HCPs
- [ ] UK English spelling throughout (colour, organise, recognised)
