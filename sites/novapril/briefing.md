# NOVAPRIL (novaprylimab) — Site Briefing

## Drug Profile

- **Brand name:** NOVAPRIL
- **Generic name:** novaprylimab
- **Class:** Fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody; first-in-class dual BAFF/APRIL pathway inhibitor
- **Indication:** Treatment of adult patients with active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) who have an inadequate response to standard therapy
- **Extended indication:** Treatment of adult patients with active lupus nephritis (LN), Class III, IV, or V (± III or IV), as an add-on to standard therapy
- **Brand colour:** Deep Teal #007B8A
- **Approval:** MHRA approved, March 2026

## Mechanism of Action

Novaprylimab selectively binds to both B-cell activating factor (BAFF) and A Proliferation-Inducing Ligand (APRIL), blocking their interaction with the receptors BCMA, TACI, and BAFF-R on B cells. This dual inhibition:

- Reduces survival signals to pathogenic autoantibody-producing B cells
- Decreases circulating autoantibodies (anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith)
- Preserves protective memory B-cell populations
- Modulates plasma cell differentiation in affected tissues

Unlike single-pathway inhibitors (e.g. belimumab targeting BAFF alone), dual BAFF/APRIL blockade addresses the compensatory upregulation of APRIL that occurs with BAFF-only inhibition, providing more complete B-cell modulation.

## Dosing

- **Formulation:** 200 mg/mL solution for injection in a pre-filled pen
- **Loading dose:** 200 mg subcutaneous injection at Weeks 0, 2, and 4
- **Maintenance dose:** 200 mg subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks
- **Administration:** Subcutaneous injection into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm
- **Self-administration:** Patients may self-inject after appropriate training
- **No dose adjustment** required for mild-to-moderate renal impairment, mild hepatic impairment, body weight, age, or sex

## Clinical Programme: LUMINOS

### LUMINOS-1 (Phase III, pivotal — SLE)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52-week, multicentre
- **Population:** 1,018 adults with moderate-to-severe active SLE (SLEDAI-2K ≥6) despite standard therapy (corticosteroids ± antimalarials ± immunosuppressants)
- **Primary endpoint:** SRI-4 response at Week 52
- **Key results:**
  - SRI-4: **61.2%** vs 40.8% placebo (OR 2.29, 95% CI: 1.74–3.02; P<0.001)
  - Severe flare reduction: **52%** (HR 0.48, 95% CI: 0.34–0.68; P<0.001)
  - Sustained corticosteroid reduction to ≤7.5 mg/day prednisone equivalent: **48.3%** vs 29.1% (OR 2.28, 95% CI: 1.71–3.05; P<0.001)
  - BICLA response at Week 52: **53.8%** vs 36.2% (P<0.001)
  - Time to first severe flare: significantly prolonged (median not reached vs 38.4 weeks)

### LUMINOS-2 (Phase III, pivotal — Lupus Nephritis)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52-week, multicentre
- **Population:** 456 adults with active lupus nephritis (Class III, IV, or V ± III or IV), biopsy-confirmed within 6 months, on standard therapy (mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide induction followed by azathioprine/mycophenolate maintenance)
- **Primary endpoint:** Complete renal response (CRR) at Week 52 (uPCR <0.5 g/g, eGFR within 10% of baseline, no rescue therapy)
- **Key results:**
  - CRR: **44.7%** vs 22.1% placebo (OR 2.87, 95% CI: 1.89–4.36; P<0.001)
  - Proteinuria reduction at Week 52: **67%** mean reduction vs 31% placebo
  - Overall renal response (ORR): **61.3%** vs 39.8% (P<0.001)
  - Sustained eGFR stability: **78.2%** vs 64.1% (P=0.002)
  - Time to CRR: median 24.1 weeks vs 38.7 weeks (P<0.001)

## Safety Profile (Pooled data, n=1,474)

### Most common adverse events (≥5%)

| Adverse Event | NOVAPRIL (n=982) | Placebo (n=492) |
|---|---|---|
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 12.3% | 10.1% |
| Injection site reactions | 8.2% | 3.1% |
| Headache | 7.4% | 6.8% |
| Nasopharyngitis | 6.1% | 5.2% |
| Urinary tract infection | 5.8% | 5.4% |
| Arthralgia | 5.3% | 5.1% |

### Key safety findings

- **Serious infections:** 4.1% vs 3.8% (mostly pneumonia, cellulitis)
- **Hypogammaglobulinaemia:** 2.8% — monitor immunoglobulin levels before initiation and periodically during treatment
- **Serious adverse events:** 8.7% vs 9.2% (numerically lower vs placebo)
- **Discontinuation due to AEs:** 3.2% vs 2.8%
- **Malignancy:** 0.4% vs 0.5% (no increased risk observed)
- **Depression/suicidal ideation:** 1.9% vs 2.1% (no increased risk)
- **Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML):** no cases reported
- **Hepatobiliary events:** no clinically significant hepatotoxicity

### Special populations

- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment required. Limited data in patients >75 years.
- **Severe renal impairment:** Use with caution; limited data available.
- **Vaccination:** Avoid live vaccines during treatment and for 4 months after discontinuation. Non-live vaccines may be administered.

#### Section 2 — The Role of B Cells in SLE
**Block:** introduction
**Section metadata:** Style: light

```
The role of B cells in SLE
Systemic lupus erythematosus is driven by a loss of B-cell tolerance, leading to the production of pathogenic autoantibodies that form immune complexes and drive tissue inflammation and organ damage.¹ ³ Two cytokines — BAFF and APRIL — play central roles in promoting the survival and differentiation of these autoreactive B cells.¹
```

#### Section 3 — BAFF Pathway
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Image:** baff-pathway.jpeg (4:3, 800×600)
**Image prompt:** "4:3 aspect ratio. Scientific illustration of the BAFF signalling pathway on a B-cell. Abstract molecular receptor (BAFF-R) on a cell surface with BAFF ligand approaching. Deep teal background with glowing white and gold molecular elements. Clean, modern pharmaceutical illustration style."

```
BAFF: B-cell activating factor
BAFF (also known as BLyS) is a key survival factor for transitional and mature B cells. Elevated BAFF levels are found in patients with active SLE and correlate with disease activity and anti-dsDNA antibody titres.¹ ³
BAFF signals through three receptors: BAFF-R (primary survival receptor on mature B cells), TACI, and BCMA. Blocking BAFF reduces survival signals to autoreactive B cells, but can lead to compensatory upregulation of APRIL signalling.¹
```

#### Section 4 — APRIL Pathway
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Section metadata:** Style: highlight
**Image:** april-pathway.jpeg (4:3, 800×600)
**Image prompt:** "4:3 aspect ratio. Scientific illustration of the APRIL signalling pathway on a B-cell. Abstract molecular receptors (TACI and BCMA) on a cell surface with APRIL ligand approaching. Deep teal and warm gold colour palette. Clean, modern pharmaceutical illustration style."

```
APRIL: a proliferation-inducing ligand
APRIL promotes the survival of plasma cells and supports immunoglobulin class switching. In SLE, APRIL contributes to the persistence of long-lived plasma cells that produce pathogenic autoantibodies, particularly in affected tissues such as the kidneys.¹
APRIL signals through TACI and BCMA. In patients treated with BAFF-only inhibitors, compensatory APRIL upregulation can sustain autoantibody-producing plasma cells, limiting the therapeutic response.¹
```

The advantage of dual BAFF/APRIL inhibition
By simultaneously blocking both BAFF and APRIL, NOVAPRIL provides more comprehensive B-cell modulation than single-pathway inhibitors.¹
Dual inhibition addresses the compensatory APRIL upregulation observed with BAFF-only blockade, reduces both transitional B-cell survival and plasma cell persistence, and leads to greater reductions in pathogenic autoantibodies and more robust clinical responses as demonstrated in the LUMINOS programme.¹ ⁴
