# REVANTHA (revantholimab) — Site Briefing

## Drug Profile

- **Brand name:** REVANTHA
- **Generic name:** revantholimab
- **Class:** Fully human IgG4 bispecific antibody; first-in-class dual PD-L1/TIGIT checkpoint inhibitor
- **Indication:** First-line treatment of adult patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) whose tumours express PD-L1 (TPS &ge;1%) with no EGFR or ALK genomic tumour aberrations, in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy
- **Extended indication:** Monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC after prior platinum-based chemotherapy, whose tumours express PD-L1 (TPS &ge;50%)
- **Brand colour:** Sapphire Blue #1B4F8A
- **Approval:** MHRA approved, February 2026

## Mechanism of Action

Revantholimab is a bispecific antibody that simultaneously binds programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and T-cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT), two co-inhibitory pathways exploited by tumours to evade immune surveillance. This dual blockade:

- Restores anti-tumour T-cell effector function by blocking PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitory signalling
- Prevents TIGIT-mediated suppression of NK cells and CD8+ T cells
- Enhances dendritic cell-mediated antigen presentation
- Promotes a pro-inflammatory tumour microenvironment favouring immune-mediated tumour rejection

Unlike sequential or combination approaches using separate agents, a single bispecific molecule ensures co-engagement of both targets on the same immune cell, providing synergistic T-cell activation with a simplified treatment regimen.

## Dosing

- **Formulation:** 20 mg/mL concentrate for solution for infusion (1200 mg/60 mL vial)
- **First-line combination:** 1200 mg intravenous infusion every 3 weeks in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for 4 cycles, followed by REVANTHA monotherapy every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity
- **Second-line monotherapy:** 1200 mg intravenous infusion every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity
- **First infusion:** Administered over 60 minutes. If tolerated, subsequent infusions may be administered over 30 minutes
- **No dose adjustment** required for body weight, mild-to-moderate renal impairment, or mild hepatic impairment

## Clinical Programme: ASCEND

### ASCEND-1 (Phase III, pivotal — First-line NSCLC + chemotherapy)

- **Design:** Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentre
- **Population:** 1,142 adults with untreated metastatic NSCLC (PD-L1 TPS &ge;1%), no EGFR/ALK alterations, ECOG PS 0&ndash;1
- **Primary endpoints:** Progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS)
- **Key results:**
  - Median PFS: **9.8 months** vs 5.4 months placebo + chemo (HR 0.56, 95% CI: 0.47&ndash;0.67; P&lt;0.001)
  - Median OS: **22.1 months** vs 14.9 months (HR 0.64, 95% CI: 0.53&ndash;0.77; P&lt;0.001)
  - Objective response rate (ORR): **52.3%** vs 33.1% (P&lt;0.001)
  - Median duration of response (DoR): 14.2 months vs 7.8 months
  - PFS benefit observed across PD-L1 TPS &ge;1%, &ge;10%, and &ge;50% subgroups

### ASCEND-2 (Phase III, pivotal — Second-line NSCLC monotherapy)

- **Design:** Randomised, open-label, active-controlled (vs docetaxel), multicentre
- **Population:** 684 adults with metastatic NSCLC progressing after platinum-based chemotherapy, PD-L1 TPS &ge;50%, ECOG PS 0&ndash;1
- **Primary endpoint:** Overall survival (OS)
- **Key results:**
  - Median OS: **18.7 months** vs 10.3 months docetaxel (HR 0.58, 95% CI: 0.46&ndash;0.73; P&lt;0.001)
  - Median PFS: 6.9 months vs 3.4 months (HR 0.52, 95% CI: 0.42&ndash;0.65; P&lt;0.001)
  - ORR: **38.6%** vs 14.2% (P&lt;0.001)
  - Median DoR: 16.1 months vs 6.2 months
  - Improved patient-reported quality of life (EORTC QLQ-C30)

| Event | REVANTHA (n=1,221) | Comparator (n=605) |
|---|---|---|
| Thyroid disorders (hypo/hyperthyroidism) | 14.8% | 3.4% |
| Pneumonitis | 5.2% | 1.1% |
| Hepatitis | 3.8% | 1.4% |
| Colitis | 2.6% | 0.8% |
| Nephritis | 1.2% | 0.3% |
| Adrenal insufficiency | 0.9% | 0.2% |

### Key safety findings

- **Immune-mediated AEs (any grade):** 32.4% vs 8.2% (mostly Grade 1&ndash;2, manageable with guidelines)
- **Grade 3&ndash;4 immune-mediated AEs:** 8.1% vs 1.9%
- **Infusion-related reactions:** 6.3% (Grade 3&ndash;4: 0.8%)
- **Serious adverse events:** 31.2% vs 34.8% (numerically lower vs comparator)
- **Treatment discontinuation due to AEs:** 9.1% vs 11.7%
- **Treatment-related deaths:** 1.4% vs 1.8%

### Special populations

- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 5 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended during treatment and for 5 months after the last dose.
- **Elderly (&ge;65 years):** No dose adjustment required. No clinically meaningful differences in safety or efficacy.
- **Hepatic impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild impairment. Not studied in moderate-to-severe.
- **Autoimmune disease:** Use with caution in patients with a history of autoimmune disease; risk of exacerbation.

```
How tumours evade the immune system
Cancer cells exploit immune checkpoint pathways to suppress T-cell and NK cell activity, creating an immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment. Two key inhibitory pathways — PD-L1 and TIGIT — act in concert to dampen anti-tumour immune responses, making simultaneous blockade a rational therapeutic strategy.¹ ³
```

#### Section 3 — PD-L1 Pathway
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Image:** pdl1-pathway.jpeg (4:3, 800x600)
**Image prompt:** "4:3 aspect ratio. Scientific illustration of the PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint pathway between a T-cell and a tumour cell.
```
PD-L1: suppressing T-cell attack
Tumour cells and antigen-presenting cells upregulate PD-L1, which binds PD-1 on T cells to deliver an inhibitory signal that suppresses T-cell proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxic activity.¹ ³
PD-L1 blockade has transformed NSCLC treatment, but many patients do not respond or develop resistance, suggesting additional inhibitory pathways limit the anti-tumour immune response.¹
```

#### Section 4 — TIGIT Pathway
**Block:** columns-teaser
**Section metadata:** Style: highlight
**Image:** tigit-pathway.jpeg (4:3, 800x600)
**Image prompt:** "4:3 aspect ratio. Scientific illustration of the TIGIT checkpoint pathway on a T-cell surface interacting with CD155 on a tumour cell. Deep sapphire blue and warm gold colour palette. Abstract immunology, glowing molecular interactions. Clean, modern pharmaceutical illustration style."

```
TIGIT: a complementary immune brake
TIGIT is an inhibitory receptor expressed on T cells and NK cells. It competes with the activating receptor CD226 (DNAM-1) for binding to CD155 and CD112 on tumour cells, tipping the balance towards immune suppression.¹
TIGIT expression is upregulated in the tumour microenvironment and correlates with T-cell exhaustion. Notably, TIGIT is often co-expressed with PD-1 on tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes, providing the rationale for dual blockade.¹
```

```
The bispecific advantage
By engineering both PD-L1 and TIGIT binding arms into a single molecule, REVANTHA ensures co-engagement of both checkpoint pathways on the same immune cell, providing synergistic T-cell reactivation.¹
This bispecific design offers advantages over combination approaches: simultaneous target engagement, enhanced avidity through co-binding, a simplified single-agent regimen, and coordinated blockade that addresses PD-L1 monotherapy resistance mechanisms.¹ ⁴
```
