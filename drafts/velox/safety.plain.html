Safety profile of VELOX

VELOX® (veloxaban) has been evaluated in over 9,400 patients across the VELOCITY clinical programme, with treatment durations of up to 24 months.¹ ² ³

A new safety paradigm in anticoagulation

By targeting Factor XIa — a key driver of pathological thrombus formation that plays a limited role in normal haemostasis — VELOX achieves a fundamentally different safety profile from established anticoagulants. In VELOCITY-AF, serious adverse events were lower with VELOX than rivaroxaban (12.4% vs 13.8%), driven primarily by fewer bleeding-related hospitalisations. Treatment discontinuation due to adverse events was low (4.8% vs 5.4%).¹ ² ³

Bleeding events

ISTH major bleeding: 1.52%/year (VELOX) vs 2.58%/year (rivaroxaban) in VELOCITY-AF (HR 0.59; P<0.001). In the high-bleeding-risk VELOCITY-BLEED population, major or CRNM bleeding was 4.12% vs 6.97% vs apixaban (HR 0.59; P<0.001). Intracranial haemorrhage was reduced by 54–60% across both trials.¹ ²

Hepatic safety

ALT elevations >3× ULN occurred in 1.1% of VELOX-treated patients across pooled VELOCITY data, comparable to 0.9% with comparators. No cases meeting Hy’s Law criteria were observed. Routine hepatic monitoring is not required; assess liver function before initiation and as clinically indicated.³

Gastrointestinal events

Dyspepsia (2.3%) and diarrhoea (2.1%) were the most common non-bleeding GI adverse events. GI major bleeding was reduced by 40% versus rivaroxaban in VELOCITY-AF (0.62%/year vs 1.04%/year; HR 0.60; P<0.001) and by 40% versus apixaban in VELOCITY-BLEED (HR 0.60; P=0.001).¹ ²

Consistent safety across key subgroups

In VELOCITY-AF, the bleeding benefit of VELOX over rivaroxaban was consistent across pre-specified subgroups including patients aged ≥75 years, those with moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30–49 mL/min), patients receiving concomitant antiplatelet therapy, and those with prior GI bleeding. In every subgroup, major bleeding rates were numerically or significantly lower with VELOX while stroke prevention efficacy was maintained.¹ ³

Prescribing Information

VELOX (veloxaban) 40 mg film-coated tablets. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. Indication: Prevention of stroke and systemic embolism in adult patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation with one or more risk factors for stroke (prior stroke/TIA/SE, age ≥75 years, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, heart failure NYHA ≥II). Dosage and administration: 40 mg once daily, with or without food. Swallow whole; do not crush, split, or chew. No dose adjustment required for age, body weight, or renal impairment (CrCl ≥30 mL/min). Contraindicated in CrCl <15 mL/min. Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to veloxaban or excipients; active clinically significant bleeding; hepatic disease associated with coagulopathy (Child-Pugh C); concomitant treatment with strong dual CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors; lesion or condition at significant risk of major bleeding; prosthetic heart valve requiring anticoagulant treatment. Warnings and precautions: Assess renal function before initiation and when clinically indicated during treatment. Discontinue if severe hepatic impairment develops. If switching from a vitamin K antagonist, discontinue VKA and start VELOX when INR <2.0. Reversal: No specific antidote; andexanet alfa is not expected to reverse veloxaban. Manage major bleeding with standard supportive measures and consider prothrombin complex concentrate. Interactions: Avoid strong dual CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir). Strong CYP3A4/P-gp inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) may reduce efficacy; avoid concomitant use. Side effects: Common (≥1/100 to <1/10): epistaxis, contusion, gingival bleeding, haematuria. Uncommon (≥1/1000 to <1/100): intracranial haemorrhage, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, ALT increase. Legal category: POM. Pack and price: 28 tablets (40 mg): £58.80; 56 tablets (40 mg): £117.60. Marketing authorisation holder: AstraZeneca UK Ltd. MA number: PLGB 17901/0721.
