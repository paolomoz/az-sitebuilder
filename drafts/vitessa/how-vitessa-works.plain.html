Targeting IL-33: the upstream alarmin driving type 2 inflammation

VITESSA neutralises interleukin-33 before it initiates the inflammatory cascade, addressing severe eosinophilic asthma at its immunological origin.1

The alarmin signal: IL-33 release from damaged airways

When airway epithelial cells are damaged by allergens, respiratory viruses, or environmental pollutants, they release IL-33 as an alarm signal. Unlike cytokines that require de novo synthesis, IL-33 is pre-formed and released rapidly, acting as an immediate danger signal to the immune system.1 3

IL-33 binds to the ST2 receptor on ILC2 cells, Th2 cells, mast cells, basophils, and eosinophils, initiating and amplifying the type 2 inflammatory response that drives severe eosinophilic asthma.1

From one signal to many: the type 2 inflammatory cascade

Once released, IL-33 triggers a coordinated inflammatory response through multiple parallel pathways. It activates ILC2 cells to produce IL-5 and IL-13, drives Th2 cell differentiation, promotes eosinophil recruitment and survival, and stimulates mast cell degranulation.1 3

This cascade results in the hallmark features of severe eosinophilic asthma: airway eosinophilia, mucus hypersecretion, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and progressive airway remodelling. Targeting downstream mediators individually leaves other branches of this cascade intact.1

VITESSA: neutralising IL-33 at the apex of the cascade

Vitessamab binds to IL-33 with high affinity and prevents its interaction with the ST2 receptor, effectively silencing the alarmin signal before it can initiate downstream inflammation.1

By acting upstream of IL-5, IL-13, and IL-4 signalling, VITESSA simultaneously suppresses eosinophil activation, mucus production, and airway remodelling through a single mechanism. This broad anti-inflammatory effect is reflected in the clinical data: deep suppression of blood eosinophils (85% reduction), substantial lung function improvement (+280 mL FEV1), and dramatic exacerbation reduction (68%) in the CLARITY-1 trial.1 4

Why IL-33 matters in severe asthma

IL-33 is released by damaged airway epithelial cells in response to allergens, viruses, pollutants, and mechanical stress. As the most upstream trigger of the type 2 inflammatory response, IL-33 activates multiple cell types and pathways simultaneously — making it a uniquely strategic therapeutic target.1 3

Prescribing Information

VITESSA (vitessamab) Prescribing Information. Please refer to the full Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. VITESSA 210 mg solution for injection in a pre-filled pen. Each pen contains 210 mg vitessamab in 1.4 mL. Indication: Add-on maintenance treatment in adults with severe eosinophilic asthma inadequately controlled despite high-dose ICS plus a LABA. Posology: 210 mg subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks. Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipient.
