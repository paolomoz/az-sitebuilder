Safety profile

Zenturigliptin has been evaluated for safety in over 5,600 patients across the ZENITH Phase III clinical programme.1–3

A well-characterised safety profile across three pivotal trials

The most commonly reported adverse events with zenturigliptin were gastrointestinal in nature. These were typically mild to moderate, occurred early in treatment, and declined with continued use. The overall discontinuation rate due to adverse events was 6.8%.1–3

Gastrointestinal tolerability

Nausea (16.2%) and diarrhoea (11.4%) were the most common adverse events. GI events were typically mild to moderate, occurring within the first 4–8 weeks and declining with continued treatment. Discontinuation due to GI events occurred in 4.2% of patients.1–3

Hepatic monitoring

Transient ALT elevations >3× ULN were observed in 3.1% of patients. Liver function tests should be performed prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Discontinue zenturigliptin if ALT >3× ULN is accompanied by symptoms or bilirubin elevation.4

Low hypoglycaemia risk

Documented hypoglycaemia occurred in only 1.2% of patients receiving zenturigliptin with metformin. Risk is increased with concomitant sulfonylureas (7.8%) or insulin (12.4%). Dose reduction of sulfonylurea or insulin may be required.1–3

Special populations

Renal impairment: No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73m²). Zenturigliptin is not recommended in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²) or end-stage renal disease due to limited clinical data.4

Hepatic impairment: No dose adjustment is required in mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A). Caution is advised in moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B). Zenturigliptin is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).4

Elderly (≥65 years): No dose adjustment is required. Clinical experience in patients aged ≥75 years is limited; use with caution in this population.4

Prescribing Information

ZENTURIS (zenturigliptin) 14 mg film-coated tablets. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing.

Indication: Treatment of adults with insufficiently controlled type 2 diabetes mellitus as an adjunct to diet and exercise.

Dosage and administration: 14 mg once weekly, taken in the morning on an empty stomach with a sip of plain water. The tablet must be swallowed whole and not crushed, chewed, or split. No food, drink, or other oral medicinal products should be taken for at least 30 minutes after administration.

Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to zenturigliptin or any excipient. Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2.

Warnings and precautions: Not recommended in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²). Monitor liver function tests prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Not a substitute for insulin. Pancreatitis: discontinue if suspected.

Pregnancy and lactation: Not recommended.

Side effects: Very common (≥1/10): nausea, diarrhoea. Common (≥1/100 to <1/10): vomiting, decreased appetite, constipation, abdominal pain, ALT increase, headache.

Legal category: POM.

Pack and price: 4 tablets (28-day supply): £185.64.

Marketing authorisation holder: AstraZeneca UK Ltd. MA number: PLGB 17901/0486.
