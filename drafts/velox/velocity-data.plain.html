The VELOCITY clinical programme

VELOX® (veloxaban) was evaluated in over 18,000 patients across two landmark Phase III trials, demonstrating non-inferior stroke prevention with significantly fewer bleeding events than established Factor Xa inhibitors.¹ ²

Redefining the anticoagulation risk–benefit balance

The VELOCITY programme was designed to evaluate whether selective Factor XIa inhibition can maintain effective stroke prevention while fundamentally reducing bleeding risk. VELOCITY-AF assessed stroke prevention in 14,228 patients with NVAF against rivaroxaban. VELOCITY-BLEED assessed bleeding safety in 4,612 high-risk AF patients against apixaban — the comparator with the lowest established bleeding rate among Factor Xa inhibitors.¹ ²

VELOCITY-AF: Stroke prevention

VELOCITY-AF: Non-inferior stroke prevention, 41% fewer major bleeds

VELOCITY-AF was a randomised, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled trial comparing VELOX 40 mg once daily with rivaroxaban 20 mg once daily in 14,228 adults with non-valvular atrial fibrillation and CHA&sub2;DS&sub2;-VASc score ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women), treated for a median of 24 months.¹

VELOX met its primary efficacy endpoint, demonstrating non-inferiority to rivaroxaban for the prevention of stroke and systemic embolism (1.68%/year vs 1.72%/year; HR 0.97; 95% CI: 0.83–1.14; P<0.001 for non-inferiority). Haemorrhagic stroke was reduced by 42% (HR 0.58; P=0.011). For the primary safety endpoint, ISTH major bleeding was reduced by 41% (1.52%/year vs 2.58%/year; HR 0.59; P<0.001). Intracranial haemorrhage was reduced by 60% (HR 0.40; P<0.001) and GI major bleeding by 40% (HR 0.60; P<0.001). The net clinical benefit composite favoured VELOX (HR 0.79; P<0.001).¹

VELOCITY-BLEED: Bleeding safety in high-risk patients

VELOCITY-BLEED: Superior bleeding safety in high-risk AF patients

VELOCITY-BLEED was a randomised, double-blind, active-controlled trial comparing VELOX 40 mg once daily with apixaban (5 mg or 2.5 mg twice daily per label) in 4,612 adults with NVAF and at least one additional bleeding risk factor (age ≥80; CrCl 30–49 mL/min; history of GI bleeding; concomitant antiplatelet; HAS-BLED ≥3) over 52 weeks.²

VELOX demonstrated a 41% reduction in the primary endpoint of ISTH major or clinically relevant non-major bleeding versus apixaban (4.12% vs 6.97%; HR 0.59; 95% CI: 0.49–0.71; P<0.001). Major bleeding alone was reduced by 41% (HR 0.59; P=0.002). Intracranial haemorrhage was reduced by 54% (HR 0.46; P=0.024). Stroke and systemic embolism rates were non-inferior to apixaban (1.82% vs 1.74%; HR 1.05; 95% CI: 0.77–1.42). The net clinical benefit favoured VELOX (HR 0.76; P<0.001).²

Prescribing Information

VELOX (veloxaban) 40 mg film-coated tablets. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. Indication: Prevention of stroke and systemic embolism in adult patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation with one or more risk factors for stroke (prior stroke/TIA/SE, age ≥75 years, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, heart failure NYHA ≥II). Dosage and administration: 40 mg once daily, with or without food. Swallow whole; do not crush, split, or chew. No dose adjustment required for age, body weight, or renal impairment (CrCl ≥30 mL/min). Contraindicated in CrCl <15 mL/min. Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to veloxaban or excipients; active clinically significant bleeding; hepatic disease associated with coagulopathy (Child-Pugh C); concomitant treatment with strong dual CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors; lesion or condition at significant risk of major bleeding; prosthetic heart valve requiring anticoagulant treatment. Warnings and precautions: Assess renal function before initiation and when clinically indicated during treatment. Discontinue if severe hepatic impairment develops. If switching from a vitamin K antagonist, discontinue VKA and start VELOX when INR <2.0. Reversal: No specific antidote; andexanet alfa is not expected to reverse veloxaban. Manage major bleeding with standard supportive measures and consider prothrombin complex concentrate. Interactions: Avoid strong dual CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir). Strong CYP3A4/P-gp inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) may reduce efficacy; avoid concomitant use. Side effects: Common (≥1/100 to <1/10): epistaxis, contusion, gingival bleeding, haematuria. Uncommon (≥1/1000 to <1/100): intracranial haemorrhage, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, ALT increase. Legal category: POM. Pack and price: 28 tablets (40 mg): £58.80; 56 tablets (40 mg): £117.60. Marketing authorisation holder: AstraZeneca UK Ltd. MA number: PLGB 17901/0721.
