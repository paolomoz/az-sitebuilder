# The MLR Review Process

## What is MLR?

**MLR** stands for **Medical, Legal, and Regulatory** — the three disciplines that must review and approve every piece of external-facing pharmaceutical content before it can be published.

In pharma, you cannot publish a webpage, a social post, a sales aid, or even a conference poster without formal sign-off from all three functions. This is not optional — it is a legal and regulatory requirement enforced by the FDA, EMA, MHRA, and other global health authorities.

The MLR review is the single biggest bottleneck in pharmaceutical marketing. It is also the single most important quality gate.

---

## Why MLR Exists

Pharmaceutical companies are not normal marketers. The content they produce can directly influence clinical decisions — which drug a doctor prescribes, which treatment a patient receives. Getting it wrong has real consequences:

- **Patient harm** from misleading efficacy claims or omitted safety information
- **Regulatory action** — warning letters, fines, forced corrections, product recalls
- **Legal liability** — lawsuits from off-label promotion or unsubstantiated claims
- **Reputational damage** — loss of trust with healthcare professionals and regulators

MLR review exists to ensure that every claim is truthful, balanced, substantiated, and compliant.

---

## The Three Reviewers

### Medical (the "M")
**Role**: Scientific accuracy and clinical integrity

Medical reviewers (typically MDs, PharmDs, or Medical Science Liaisons) verify that:
- Every clinical claim is supported by published evidence (clinical trials, peer-reviewed papers)
- Data is presented accurately — no cherry-picking, no misleading statistics
- Safety information (adverse events, contraindications, warnings) is complete and prominent
- The content is consistent with the approved product label / SmPC
- No off-label claims are made (promoting uses not approved by regulators)
- Fair balance is maintained — benefits and risks are given proportionate weight

### Legal (the "L")
**Role**: Legal compliance and risk mitigation

Legal reviewers verify that:
- Content complies with advertising and promotion laws (country-specific)
- Intellectual property is respected (trademarks, copyrights, third-party content)
- Comparative claims against competitors are defensible
- Privacy regulations are observed (especially for patient-facing content)
- Terms and conditions, disclaimers, and legal notices are present
- The company's legal exposure is minimised

### Regulatory (the "R")
**Role**: Regulatory compliance and submission readiness

Regulatory reviewers verify that:
- Content aligns with the approved prescribing information / SmPC
- Required regulatory elements are present (approval codes, date of preparation, PI references)
- Content meets the specific requirements of the target market's regulatory body
- Materials are ready for submission to regulators if requested
- Adverse event reporting mechanisms are properly referenced
- All required fair balance and risk information is included

## What Goes Through MLR?

Virtually **all external-facing content** requires MLR review:

| Content Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Digital** | HCP websites, patient portals, email campaigns, banner ads |
| **Social** | LinkedIn posts, X/Twitter, conference social content |
| **Print** | Detail aids, leave-behinds, journal ads, posters |
| **Sales** | Rep-triggered emails, CRM content, presentation decks |
| **Medical** | Medical education materials, congress materials |
| **Video** | MOA animations, KOL interviews, webinar content |
| **Events** | Booth panels, speaker slides, handouts |

Internal-only content (e.g. training materials) typically goes through a lighter review, but anything that could reach an external audience needs full MLR.

## The MLR Workflow

### Typical Stages

```
1. CREATION        Marketing/Medical Affairs creates content
       ↓
2. SUBMISSION      Content submitted to MLR review system with:
                   - Draft content (document, webpage, video, etc.)
                   - References supporting each claim
                   - Target audience and channel
                   - Intended market(s)
                   ↓
3. REVIEW          Each reviewer (M, L, R) reviews independently:
                   - Annotates issues directly on the content
                   - Classifies each comment (mandatory change, suggestion, query)
                   - May approve, approve with changes, or reject
                   ↓
4. CONSOLIDATION   Review comments are consolidated
                   - Conflicts between reviewers are resolved
                   - Priority of changes is determined
                   ↓
5. REVISION        Content creator addresses all comments
                   - Mandatory changes are implemented
                   - Responses to queries are provided
                   - Revised content is resubmitted
                   ↓
6. RE-REVIEW       Reviewers verify their comments were addressed
                   (may trigger another revision cycle)
                   ↓
7. APPROVAL        All three functions sign off
                   - Approval code is assigned (e.g., "GB-12345 | DOP: March 2026")
                   - Expiry date is set (typically 1-2 years)
                   - Approved content is locked
                   ↓
8. PUBLICATION     Content goes live
                   ↓
9. MONITORING      Post-publication surveillance
                   - Track if content needs updating (new safety data, label changes)
                   - Manage expiry and re-approval
```

### Typical Timelines

| Scenario | Duration |
|---|---|
| Simple content update (text change to approved material) | 1-2 weeks |
| New webpage or digital asset | 3-6 weeks |
| Full campaign launch (multiple assets) | 8-16 weeks |
| Complex content with new data or comparative claims | 6-12 weeks |
| Re-approval of expiring content (no changes) | 1-3 weeks |

These timelines are a major pain point. A single HCP webpage that takes a developer 2 hours to build may spend 6 weeks in MLR review cycles.

## Key Artefacts

### Approval Code
Every piece of approved content receives a unique identifier:
- Format varies by company, e.g. `GB-12345` or `MATS-2026-0042`
- Always displayed on the published material
- Links back to the full approval record in the review system

### Date of Preparation (DOP)
The date the content was prepared/approved. Required on all materials.
Displayed alongside the approval code, e.g. `GB-12345 | DOP: March 2026`

### References
Every clinical claim must be traceable to a specific reference:
- Published clinical trials
- Product SmPC / Prescribing Information
- Data on file
- Peer-reviewed publications

References are typically numbered and listed at the bottom of the material.

### Claims Library
Many pharma companies maintain a **pre-approved claims library** — a database of statements that have already passed MLR review. Using pre-approved claims can dramatically accelerate review.

### Annotations
The currency of MLR review. Reviewers mark up content with:
- **Mandatory changes** — must be implemented before approval
- **Suggestions** — recommended but not blocking
- **Queries** — questions requiring a response from the content creator

## Current Pain Points

1. **Slow cycle times** — Multiple rounds of review and revision stretch timelines to weeks or months
2. **Manual, email-driven workflows** — Many companies still route content via email, SharePoint, or disconnected systems
3. **Version confusion** — Multiple versions circulating simultaneously, unclear which is current
4. **Reviewer bottleneck** — Medical and regulatory reviewers are scarce resources shared across many brands
5. **Repetitive reviews** — Similar content reviewed from scratch each time instead of leveraging prior approvals
6. **Channel explosion** — Digital, social, web, video, email — more content types requiring review than ever
7. **Global complexity** — Same content adapted for multiple markets, each with different regulatory requirements
8. **Disconnect between content creation and review** — Content is created in one system, reviewed in another, published in a third

## The Technology Landscape

### Current Tools
- **Veeva Vault PromoMats** — Dominant MLR review platform in pharma (used by most top-20 pharma companies)
- **Veeva Vault MedComms** — Medical content variant
- **Zinc Ahead / IQVIA** — Alternative review platforms
- **Adobe Workfront** — Sometimes used for creative workflow upstream of MLR

### Emerging AI Applications (2025-2026)
- **Pre-submission AI checks** — Flag potential issues before human review (off-label language, missing safety info, unsupported claims)
- **Claims matching** — Automatically match content claims to reference library
- **Annotation AI** — Suggest reviewer comments based on historical patterns
- **Content reuse detection** — Identify when submitted content matches previously approved materials
- **Translation QA** — Verify translated content preserves medical accuracy
