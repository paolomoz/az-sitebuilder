The AURORA clinical programme

AXURA®▼ (axurelimab) was evaluated in two Phase III trials encompassing the two major clinical presentations of lupus: systemic disease (AURORA-1) and renal involvement (AURORA-LN).1 2

64% reduction in anti-dsDNA antibodies at Week 52

AXURA produced a median 64.1% reduction in anti-dsDNA titres from baseline at Week 52, compared with 8.3% in the placebo group (P<0.001). Rapid reductions were observed from Week 4, consistent with depletion of autoantibody-secreting plasma cells.1

73% of patients achieved complement normalisation

C3 normalisation was achieved in 72.8% of AXURA-treated patients with low baseline C3, compared with 31.4% with placebo (P<0.001). Complement recovery correlated with clinical response and reduced flare risk.1

68% achieved glucocorticoid reduction to ≤7.5 mg/day

Among patients receiving >7.5 mg/day prednisolone at baseline, 68.2% of AXURA patients achieved sustained dose reduction to ≤7.5 mg/day by Week 52, versus 42.1% with placebo (OR 2.93; P<0.001) — reducing cumulative steroid burden.1

AURORA-1: Systemic SLE

AURORA-1: Clinically meaningful disease control across organ systems

AURORA-1 was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 768 adults with active seropositive SLE (SLEDAI-2K ≥6) despite standard of care. AXURA achieved the primary endpoint of SRI-4 response at Week 52 in 61.7% vs 35.9% (Δ 25.8%; P<0.001). BICLA response was achieved in 54.4% vs 30.5% (P<0.001). Severe flares (BILAG A) were reduced by 62% (HR 0.38; P<0.001). Notably, clinical response was accompanied by rapid and sustained normalisation of serological biomarkers (anti-dsDNA, complement), supporting a disease-modifying rather than purely symptomatic mechanism.1

AURORA-LN: Lupus nephritis

AURORA-LN: Doubling the rate of complete renal response

AURORA-LN was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 354 adults with biopsy-proven active lupus nephritis (Class III, IV, or V) on background MMF + glucocorticoids. AXURA nearly doubled the rate of complete renal response at Week 52: 43.5% vs 22.0% (Δ 21.5%; P<0.001). The overall renal response rate was 71.2% vs 49.2% (P<0.001). Renal flares were reduced by 69% (HR 0.31; P=0.001). Proteinuria (uPCR) decreased by 71.4% with AXURA vs 38.2% with placebo.2

Prescribing Information

AXURA▼ (axurelimab) 300 mg/2 mL solution for subcutaneous injection in pre-filled syringe. Please refer to the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) before prescribing. Indication: Add-on therapy for the treatment of adult patients with active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) who are seropositive (anti-dsDNA positive and/or low complement) and have an inadequate response to standard therapy. Also indicated as add-on therapy for active lupus nephritis (Class III, IV, or V) receiving standard immunosuppressive treatment. Dosage and administration: 300 mg SC every 2 weeks for the first 3 doses (loading), then 300 mg SC every 4 weeks (maintenance). Administer into the thigh, abdomen (excluding 5 cm around the navel), or upper arm. Rotate injection sites. Contraindications: Hypersensitivity to axurelimab or excipients; active severe infections; active hepatitis B infection. Warnings and precautions: Infections: Do not initiate during active infections. Screen for TB and HBV before initiation. Vaccinate against herpes zoster where possible. Immunoglobulins: Monitor IgG, IgA, IgM before initiation and regularly during treatment. Consider withholding if IgG <4.0 g/L with recurrent serious infections. Live vaccines: Avoid during treatment and for 6 months after last dose. Side effects: Very common (≥1/10): upper respiratory tract infection, injection site reactions, UTI, nasopharyngitis. Common (≥1/100 to <1/10): herpes zoster, headache, diarrhoea, nausea, arthralgia, bronchitis, IgG decrease. Legal category: POM. Pack and price: 1 pre-filled syringe (300 mg): £872.00.
