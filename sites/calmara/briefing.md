## 2. Mechanism of Action

CALMARA (calmazetine) is a first-in-class oral selective antagonist of the arginine vasopressin V1b receptor (AVPR1B). Its mechanism is entirely distinct from monoaminergic, GABAergic, and serotonergic pathways.^1

### The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis in GAD

Arginine vasopressin (AVP) acts synergistically with corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) at the pituitary V1b receptor to amplify ACTH and cortisol secretion in response to stress. Dysregulation of AVP/V1b signalling has been identified in chronic stress models and in post-mortem studies of individuals with anxiety and affective disorders.^1,2

In patients with GAD, sustained HPA axis hyperactivation contributes to the persistent, excessive worry and somatic symptoms that define the condition. Current pharmacotherapies — SSRIs, SNRIs, buspirone — act on monoaminergic transmission but do not directly address this neuroendocrine component.^2

### How CALMARA works

- Selectively antagonises V1b receptors at the pituitary, reducing stress-axis hyperactivity^1
- Reduces ACTH and cortisol secretion driven by AVP/CRH synergy^1
- Does not act on V1a or V2 receptors at therapeutic doses, which is the basis for its favourable fluid-balance and cardiovascular safety profile^1
- Does not produce sedation, receptor-level dependence, or cognitive dulling associated with GABAergic agents^1
- Mechanism is non-monoaminergic — no serotonergic side effects, no sexual dysfunction class effect^1

### Key differentiation

Calmazetine is not a sedative. It is not associated with dependence, withdrawal, or rebound anxiety. This distinction is clinically relevant for prescribers managing patients who drive, operate machinery, or require cognitive clarity at work.^1

## 3. Clinical Programme

### The SERENITY Phase III Programme

The CALMARA approval is supported by the **SERENITY programme** — two Phase III, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials conducted across 74 sites in North America and Western Europe.^3,4

### SERENITY-1 and SERENITY-2 (pooled analysis)

- **Design**: Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **Population**: 614 adults with a confirmed DSM-5 diagnosis of GAD, HAM-A total score >=18 at baseline, and documented inadequate response to at least one SSRI or SNRI (defined as failure to achieve remission [HAM-A <=7] after >=8 weeks of treatment at an adequate dose)
- **Exclusions**: Current or recent benzodiazepine use (within 4 weeks), active substance use disorder, primary diagnosis of major depressive disorder
- **Randomisation**: 1:1 to CALMARA 120 mg once daily or matching placebo
- **Duration**: 24 weeks
- **Primary endpoint**: Proportion of patients achieving >=50% reduction in HAM-A total score at Week 24
- **Key secondary endpoints**: HAM-A remission, GAD-7 minimal anxiety, CGI-I response, PROMIS Anxiety improvement, SDS functional improvement
- **Publication**: [PLACEHOLDER — requires reference details]

### Primary endpoint

| Endpoint | CALMARA | Placebo | P-value |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| HAM-A total score >=50% reduction at Week 24 | 62.4% | 24.7% | p < 0.0001 |

### Key secondary endpoints (Week 24)

| Endpoint | CALMARA | Placebo | P-value |
|----------|---------|---------|---------|
| HAM-A remission (score <=7) | 41.8% | 13.2% | p < 0.0001 |
| GAD-7 score <=4 (minimal anxiety) | 54.3% | 19.1% | p < 0.0001 |
| CGI-I score 1 or 2 (much/very much improved) | 66.7% | 28.4% | p < 0.0001 |
| PROMIS Anxiety T-score improvement >=10 pts | 48.9% | 14.6% | p < 0.0001 |
| SDS total score improvement >=40% | 55.1% | 20.3% | p < 0.0001 |

HAM-A = Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale; GAD-7 = Generalised Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale; CGI-I = Clinical Global Impression — Improvement; PROMIS = Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System; SDS = Sheehan Disability Scale. Data represent pooled ITT population (N=614). All p-values are for the primary and key secondary endpoints after pre-specified multiplicity correction.

### Onset of effect

Statistically significant separation from placebo on HAM-A total score was observed from **Week 2** in SERENITY-1 and **Week 3** in SERENITY-2. A HAM-A reduction of >=4 points (minimum clinically important difference) was achieved by 38.1% of the CALMARA arm at Week 4, versus 14.9% in the placebo arm.^3,4

The onset profile is notably faster than observed in registrational data for SSRIs in GAD, though cross-trial comparisons should be presented with appropriate methodological caveats.

### Long-term data (open-label extension)

An open-label extension (OLE) of SERENITY-1 followed 241 patients for an additional 52 weeks. HAM-A response was maintained in 78.4% of patients who entered the extension phase. No new safety signals were identified over 76 weeks of total exposure.^5

Importantly, no clinically significant cortisol axis suppression was observed — a theoretical concern given the mechanism — with 24-hour urinary free cortisol levels remaining within normal reference ranges throughout.^5

## 4. Safety Profile

The safety profile of CALMARA was consistent with its pharmacology. No signal for dependence, withdrawal, or rebound anxiety was observed on discontinuation. No clinically meaningful effects on blood pressure, serum sodium, or renal function were identified, consistent with the V1b selectivity of calmazetine.^3,4

### Adverse events (pooled SERENITY-1 and SERENITY-2)

| Adverse event | CALMARA | Placebo |
|---------------|---------|---------|
| Somnolence (transient) | 8.4% | 3.1% |
| Nausea | 7.2% | 3.6% |
| Headache | 6.5% | 5.8% |
| Dizziness | 4.9% | 2.0% |
| Fatigue | 4.1% | 3.9% |
| Dry mouth | 3.6% | 1.6% |
| Discontinuation due to AE | 4.2% | 2.6% |

### Notable safety findings

**Somnolence**: Occurred predominantly within the first 2 weeks of treatment and was largely self-resolving. Discontinuation due to somnolence was reported in 1.6% of the CALMARA arm. CALMARA is not a sedative — somnolence was transient and did not reflect the sustained sedation profile of GABAergic agents.^3,4

**No dependence or withdrawal**: No signal for dependence, withdrawal symptoms, or rebound anxiety was observed upon discontinuation in either the 24-week controlled period or the 76-week OLE.^3,4,5

**Cortisol axis safety**: Despite the V1b mechanism, no clinically significant cortisol axis suppression was observed in the OLE. 24-hour urinary free cortisol levels remained within normal reference ranges throughout 76 weeks of treatment. This addresses a predictable prescriber concern and the data are reassuring.^5

**Cardiovascular and renal**: No clinically meaningful effects on blood pressure, serum sodium, or renal function — consistent with selectivity for V1b over V1a (vascular) and V2 (renal) receptors.^1

## 12. Imagery Direction (for MLR context)

Per the original briefing, all imagery must:

- Communicate **competence, scientific rigour, and understanding** of the patient population — without romanticising suffering or stripping patients of dignity
- Show patients in contexts suggesting **restored function**: working, in conversation, in ordinary daily life
- **Avoid** imagery that codes explicitly as "anxiety": hunched posture, averted gaze, isolation, head-holding, dark sky silhouettes
- Reflect **diverse demographics** proportional to GAD prevalence (age, gender, ethnicity) — an all-white 35-50 professional cohort is clinically inaccurate
- Use **dermatology-grade clinical settings** for HCP imagery — professional and attentive, not performatively warm
- Keep MoA illustrations **clean, proportionate, scientifically plausible** — no geometric "neural network" abstractions
- Maintain a **restrained colour palette**: off-white/light grey base, dark slate or navy text, one restrained accent
- **Avoid** high-saturation wellness palettes, gradient backgrounds, and consumer advertising aesthetics
- **Whitespace is deliberate** — generous spacing communicates clinical professionalism
